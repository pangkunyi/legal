Privacy Policy

Mathdoku · published by zowle
Effective 12 November 2026

The short version. Mathdoku has no account and no sign-in. It has an online leaderboard and it shows ads. Your score, the nickname and the country you chose go to our own leaderboard server, and the advertising identifier that Google's ad service needs goes to Google — the same identifier that keeps your leaderboard entry together. Everything else — the puzzle you are working on, your notes, your timer, your best times, your settings — stays on your device and is never uploaded.

1. Who this policy covers

This policy describes the Mathdoku mobile app for Android and iOS (Android package id com.zowle.mathdoku), published by zowle. It says what the app sends, who receives it, how long it is kept and how to have it removed.

2. What leaves your device

The app has no account, so it never asks for a login, an email address or a phone number. Four things are sent, and all of them are sent so that the leaderboard can show your entry:

  • Your score — the total of the points of every puzzle you have solved. The app sends it when it is better than the score it sent last time.
  • Your nickname — the name you type in Settings, up to 20 characters. If you leave it empty, the app uses the default name NoName.
  • Your country or region — the country you pick in Settings, or the region part of your device's locale if you have not picked one. This is an approximate location and nothing more: the app never asks for GPS, never asks for a precise location and declares no location permission at all.
  • The advertising identifier — Google's Advertising ID on Android, obtained through the permission com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID. It is a resettable identifier that Google issues for advertising.

Two things worth knowing about the identifier

The app reads it lazily: it asks for the identifier when the leaderboard needs it, not when the app starts, and the game never waits for it. Google's own ad SDK reads it on its own, on the other hand, as soon as you open a puzzle, because that is how it selects and measures ads.

If the Advertising ID is not available — you reset it, you turned off ad personalisation, you are in a region where Google does not issue one, or the device has no Google Play services — the app makes an identifier of its own: a time-based UUID (version 1) generated on the device. The game plays exactly the same either way.

The identifier is cached on the device. If you reset your Advertising ID later, your leaderboard entry does not move: the app keeps using the identifier it already has, because that is what tells the server that the new score belongs to the same player.

The app does not write the identifier, your nickname or your score into the log of the device either.

3. Who receives it

One company, and only one: zowle, the publisher of this app.

  • Our own leaderboard server at gs.zowle.com. We run that server ourselves, so the leaderboard data stays with us and is not handed on to anybody else. The app talks to it over HTTPS: GET /leaderboard/kk downloads the public list, and POST /leaderboard/kk/submit sends your score, your nickname, your country and the identifier.
  • Google LLC, as the provider of the advertising through AdMob (AdMob application id ca-app-pub-6321505663126261~8041968286). Google receives the advertising identifier together with the request data it needs to select and measure ads, and handles that data under its own privacy policy. Google is the only outside party that receives anything from the app.

There is no other recipient: no analytics service, no crash-reporting service, no social SDK and no payment SDK are part of the app, and the leaderboard data does not leave our own server.

4. The leaderboard is public

The leaderboard shows the hundred best players. Every entry shows the nickname, the score and the country or region — that part is public information, and anyone can read it.

The download itself carries no identifier. The request contains your IP address and the user agent, exactly like the request for any web page. But the list that comes back contains the identifier of every entry, so that the app can tell which row is yours and highlight it. The app uses that identifier only to find your own row; it never shows it and never sends it anywhere else.

5. What stays on your device

To remember your settings and your progress, the app writes the following to its private preferences storage (Android SharedPreferences). It never leaves the device, and no other app can read it:

  • your current score and the score you submitted last;
  • the puzzle you are working on, including the numbers you have entered, the notes you wrote on the cells and the time you have spent on it;
  • the size of the grid, the operators you enabled and the difficulty you picked;
  • your colour scheme and the language of the interface;
  • your best time, your best score and your best stars, for every combination of grid size and operators.

These are your own game records rather than personal data about you. They are stored so that the app can show them back to you.

6. Backups

The app excludes its preferences from the automatic backup of Android. That data is therefore not uploaded to Google Drive, and it is not copied onto a new phone or tablet by the device-to-device transfer. A backup of Mathdoku holds no settings and no scores.

7. Advertising

A banner ad sits at the bottom of the puzzle screen while you play.

There is one more ad, and it only ever appears because you asked for it: the button with the arrow in the top row of the puzzle screen watches a short full-screen ad, and the game then fills one correct number into the board for you as a hint. You may watch one of those per minute, and the button shows the time that is left until the next one. The game never shows such an ad on its own — there is no ad when you finish a puzzle and none when you return to the game. The puzzle is always playable without watching anything: the hint is a help, never a condition.

The ads are served by Google AdMob. You stay in control of the identifier they use: you can reset or delete your Advertising ID, or turn off ad personalisation, in your device settings, and the app falls back to the local identifier described in section 2.

8. What is not collected

The app does not collect, and never has access to:

  • precise location;
  • health and fitness data;
  • financial information;
  • messages, photos and videos, audio files, files and documents;
  • calendar information and contacts;
  • web browsing history and information from other apps;
  • crash logs, diagnostics, app information and performance data;
  • the Android device identifier.

There is no account, no sign-in, no email address and no phone number anywhere in the app.

9. How long we keep it

  • Leaderboard entries are kept for as long as the leaderboard is published. You can ask for your entry to be removed at any time — see section 10.
  • Data on your device stays on your device until you clear it or uninstall the app.
  • Deletion requests are answered within 30 days.

10. Deleting your data

What lives on your device, you delete yourself:

  • on Android, Settings → Apps → Mathdoku → Storage → Clear data, or uninstall the app;
  • on iOS, Settings → General → iPhone Storage → Mathdoku → Delete App;
  • if you use Android backup, remove the backup of the app in the backup settings of the system as well.

A leaderboard entry is removed on request. Write to pangkunyi@gmail.com and tell us which entry to delete.

One detail matters: the nickname is not unique, so a request has to include the nickname and the score shown next to it on the leaderboard (your best score is fine). Without both, we cannot tell which of several players with the same nickname you mean. We remove the entry within 30 days of the request and confirm it by email.

11. iOS

On iOS the advertising identifier (IDFA) is only available if you allow it. The iOS build of the app does not ask for tracking permission and does not carry a tracking usage description, so if you do not allow the identifier the app falls back to a local identifier of its own, exactly as it does on Android. The leaderboard works the same way on both systems.

12. Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and is not listed in any Families or children category on Google Play. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, contact us and we will remove the entry.

13. Your rights

Depending on where you live, regulations such as the GDPR and the CCPA give you rights over the personal data an organisation holds about you: the right to see it, to correct it, to export it and to have it erased.

For the data on your device you already have that control — it sits in the private storage of the app, and clearing the app's data or uninstalling it removes everything. For a leaderboard entry, write to us and we will remove it within 30 days. We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it beyond the two recipients named in section 3.

14. Security

Everything the app sends travels over HTTPS, so it is encrypted in transit. The leaderboard server is reached over TLS, and the ad traffic is encrypted the same way. The data on your device is protected by the sandbox of the operating system and by the lock screen of your device.

15. Changes to this policy

If a later version of the app changes what it collects or sends, this page is updated before that version is distributed, and the effective date above changes with it. Material changes are also described in the release notes of the app.
